# Knots: Principles, Judgment, and the Working Ten · Course Syllabus

> This syllabus defines everything you will be able to do after completing this course.
> Learning depth: Deep
> The number of lessons varies by learner, but the mastery items do not get watered down.
>
> **This is a physical skill with a theoretical spine.** The documents build the spine — the mechanics, the taxonomy, the judgment, the memory system. The hands are yours: every lesson from 03 onward assumes you have cord in front of you while you read. See [kit.md](./kit.md) for the five-minute setup.

## Core Mastery Items

After completing this course, you will be able to:

### 1. The Physics — why a knot holds, and why it breaks
- [ ] Able to explain, using friction and wrap angle (the capstan relationship), why holding power grows *exponentially* rather than linearly with the number of turns — and use that to predict which of two arrangements will hold better before tying either
- [ ] Able to explain where and why a loaded knot breaks (curvature, differential fiber strain, the entry point), state the typical strength-loss range for knotted rope, and rank two unfamiliar knots by likely strength from their geometry alone
- [ ] Able to state the three properties every knot trades off — **security, strength, releasability** — and diagnose which one any given knot was optimized for, including the ones you already use without thinking

### 2. The Grammar — elements, families, and ancestry
- [ ] Able to name and identify the structural elements (standing part, working end, bight, loop, turn, round turn, half hitch, nip) in any knot, tied or diagrammed
- [ ] Able to classify any knot into its family — **stopper, bend, hitch, loop, binding, friction hitch** — from its function, and state what question each family answers
- [ ] Able to trace the ancestry of the common knots back to two roots (the overhand and the half hitch), and explain why so many knots are variations on a very small number of moves
- [ ] Able to explain why handedness (chirality) changes a knot's behavior — using reef vs. granny as the worked case — and check your own tying for it

### 3. Judgment — failure modes and when not to trust a knot
- [ ] Able to describe the main failure modes (slipping, capsizing, jamming, ring-loading, cyclic-loading loosening) and name a knot that is characteristically vulnerable to each
- [ ] Able to explain **dressing and setting**, and demonstrate why an undressed correct knot can be more dangerous than a wrong one
- [ ] Able to state how rope material and construction (nylon vs. polyester vs. polypropylene vs. HMPE, laid vs. braid vs. kernmantle) change which knots you can trust, and why slick modern cordage broke some traditional knots
- [ ] Able to draw the line honestly around life-critical loads: which decisions belong to a qualified instructor and a rated system rather than to this course

### 4. The Working Ten — coverage of 95% of real situations
- [ ] Able to tie all ten knots correctly, from memory, without visual reference — including behind your back and with the rope oriented the "wrong" way
- [ ] Able to justify each of the ten as the *marginal* addition it is: what capability it adds that the previous nine don't, and which common knot it makes unnecessary
- [ ] Able to walk into an unplanned situation (a load on a roof rack, a tarp in wind, a dog, a snapped drawstring, a bundle, a swing) and select the right knot with a stated reason in under ten seconds

### 5. Encoding and Retention — never confusing them again
- [ ] Able to state, for each of the ten, its **discriminating feature** — the one visible or tactile difference that separates it from its nearest confusable neighbor (bowline/sheet bend, clove/constrictor, reef/granny/thief, figure-8/overhand)
- [ ] Able to run a deliberate practice protocol built on retrieval, interleaving, and spacing rather than repetition — and explain why massed repetition of one knot produces the illusion of mastery
- [ ] Able to convert the whole set into your own RemNote-style card system, matching card form to knowledge type (recognition, discrimination, procedure, judgment), including the cards that a flashcard *can't* carry and what replaces them

## Not Covered in This Course

- **Life-critical rope systems** — climbing anchors, rescue rigging, arborist work, rope access. The course gives you the mechanics and vocabulary to be a better student of those disciplines; it does not qualify you to rig a system that holds a human being. That requires hands-on instruction and rated, inspected equipment.
- Mathematical knot theory (topology, knot invariants, the Jones polynomial) — a different subject that shares a name; the physical knots here are all "unknots" to a topologist
- Splicing, whipping, and rope work as craft (except the minimum end-treatment needed to keep your practice cord usable)
- Decorative and marlinspike work — Turk's heads, monkey's fists, macramé
- The long tail of specialist knots: fishing knots, surgical knots, sailing-specific rigging, net-making. We cover the *judgment* that lets you evaluate one when you meet it.
- Load ratings and engineering calculations for specific commercial cordage
