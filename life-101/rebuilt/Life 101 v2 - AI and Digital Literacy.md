- ## What a Language Model Actually Is
    - The folk belief about AI chatbots, and why it is wrong::People assume the model looks things up in a database and reports what it finds. It does not. There is no lookup and no stored library of facts to consult — the answer is generated, not retrieved. #volatile
    - What a large language model actually does::It predicts likely continuations of text, one piece at a time, using statistical structure absorbed from an enormous amount of writing. Everything it produces is a plausible continuation, and truth is not a separate step in the process.
    - Why an LLM is not a database::A database stores records and returns them unchanged. A model stores patterns and generates something new each time, which is why the same question can produce different answers and why it can produce a fluent answer about something that does not exist.
    - Why an LLM is not a mind::It has no persistent goals between conversations, no body, no ongoing experience, and by default no memory of you. Whether anything at all is going on inside is genuinely unsettled — but the confident everyday claim in either direction outruns the evidence. Contested: #theory
    - Why models sound so confident regardless of accuracy::Confident, fluent prose is the most common register in the training text, so fluency is what gets predicted. Tone is a property of the writing style, not a signal about correctness — a model is exactly as smooth when it is wrong.
    - The single most useful thing to internalize about AI output::Its confidence carries no information about its accuracy. Treat certainty in the wording as decoration, and verify anything that matters regardless of how sure it sounds.

- ## Hallucination
    - What "hallucination" means in AI::When a model produces something fluent, specific, and false — an invented statistic, a misattributed quotation, a citation to a paper that was never written.
    - Why hallucination is intrinsic rather than a bug awaiting a patch::The system generates plausible continuations. A fabricated citation is plausible — it has the right shape, the right author style, the right journal cadence — so the mechanism that produces good answers is the same one that produces convincing false ones. Mitigations reduce the rate; they do not remove the cause.
    - Where hallucination is most likely::Specific verifiable details — names, dates, numbers, page references, case citations, quotations, URLs. The more precise and checkable the claim, the more attention it deserves.
    - The fabricated-citation failure mode::Models produce citations that look perfectly formed and do not exist, including plausible authors, journals, and years. Lawyers have been sanctioned for filing them. Never repeat a citation you have not opened yourself. #safety
    - The verification discipline, stated as a rule::Never pass along a fact, a number, or a source you have not independently confirmed. The model is a drafting tool and a thinking partner; it is not a source, and it cannot be cited.
    - Why asking the model "are you sure?" does not verify anything::You are asking the same generator to predict the continuation of a challenge, which usually produces either an apology or a reassertion. Neither is evidence. Verification has to come from outside the system.

- ## What AI Is Genuinely Good and Bad At
    - What AI is genuinely good at::Drafting and rewriting, summarizing text you supply, translating, explaining a concept at a chosen level, brainstorming quantity, coding assistance, and being a tireless patient tutor that never sighs at the tenth version of the same question. #volatile
    - What AI is genuinely bad at::Anything requiring it to know whether something is true, current events beyond its training, precise arithmetic and counting, knowing what it does not know, and any situation where being confidently wrong is expensive. #volatile
    - The asymmetry that should govern when you use it::Use AI where errors are cheap and visible — a first draft you will read, code you will run and test. Avoid relying on it where errors are expensive and invisible — medical, legal, and financial specifics you would act on without checking. #safety
    - Why AI is unusually good for learning specifically::It will explain the same idea five different ways without impatience, meet you at your exact level, and let you ask the question you are embarrassed to ask a person. That is the 2-sigma tutoring advantage available on demand.
    - Why "the model agreed with me" means nothing::Models are trained to be agreeable and tend to follow the framing they are given. Agreement is weak evidence at best. If you want the objection, you have to ask for the strongest case against your position explicitly.

- ## Using It Well
    - The three things that actually improve a prompt::1) Say what you want it to do specifically, 2) give it the relevant material rather than making it recall, 3) tell it the audience and the form. Elaborate incantations matter far less than supplying context.
    - Why supplying the source text beats asking from memory::A model summarizing a document you paste is working from something real and checkable. A model recalling that document is generating a plausible version of it. Paste the text whenever you have it.
    - The best question to ask an AI about your own plan::"What is the strongest argument against this, and what would have to be true for it to fail?" It counters both your confirmation bias and the model's agreeableness in one move.
    - What you should never paste into a chatbot::Passwords, account and card numbers, government identifiers, other people's private information, medical records you do not want stored, and anything under a confidentiality obligation. Assume anything you type may be retained or reviewed. #safety
    - How to teach a kid to use AI as a tutor rather than a ghostwriter::Ask it to explain, quiz, or check reasoning — never to produce the finished thing. The test is simple: could you now explain this to someone else without the tool? If not, the tool did the learning.
    - Why using AI to write the thing costs more than it saves in school::The struggle to compose is where the understanding forms, so outsourcing it removes the entire benefit while leaving the grade intact. The grade was never the point.

- ## Deepfakes, Voice Cloning, and Scams
    - What voice cloning makes possible now::A convincing imitation of a specific person's voice from a very short sample of their real speech — enough for a phone call that sounds exactly like a family member in trouble. #safety #volatile
    - Why a familiar voice is no longer identification::Recognizing someone's voice used to be strong evidence it was them. Cloning has removed that, which means any request for money or urgent action needs verification through a separate channel, no matter who it sounds like. #safety
    - The family verification password — what it is::A phrase agreed on in advance, known only to your household, that anyone claiming an emergency by phone or video must supply. Choose something not guessable and not posted anywhere online. #safety
    - The family verification password — why it works::It is pre-commitment against a moment engineered to destroy judgment. In a real emergency you cannot reason carefully, so the decision must already be made: no password, no money, call back on a number you have. #safety
    - The grandparent scam, in its current form::A call claiming a grandchild is in jail, in an accident, or stranded abroad, needing money immediately and asking you not to tell the parents. The secrecy request is the tell. Cloned audio has made this dramatically more convincing. #safety
    - The universal scam signature, regardless of technology::Urgency, secrecy, and an unusual payment method — gift cards, wire transfer, crypto, or payment apps. No legitimate institution combines those three, and the pressure exists specifically to prevent you from checking. #safety
    - Why AI has made phishing harder to spot::Bad grammar and awkward phrasing used to be the reliable giveaway, and generated text no longer has them. Judge by the request and the channel — does this message ask me to do something unusual, and did I verify it independently? #safety
    - What to do with any unexpected message asking for money or credentials::Stop, do not use any contact detail contained in the message, and reach the person or institution through a number or address you already had. The single habit of independent callback defeats most of this category. #safety
    - Deepfake video and images — the honest current state::Convincing fakes are cheap and easy to produce, and visual artifacts are an unreliable detector and getting worse. Provenance matters more than appearance: who published this, do independent outlets have it, and does the original source exist? #volatile

- ## AI and the Information Environment
    - What "AI slop" means::Mass-produced, low-quality generated content published to capture search traffic and ad revenue. It is fluent, superficially responsive, and often subtly wrong.
    - How AI slop degrades searching for answers::It crowds out human-written material in search results, and because it is confident and well-formatted it is hard to distinguish at a glance. Prefer primary sources, named authors, and institutions with something to lose. #volatile
    - Why AI summaries of search results deserve extra suspicion::They compress sources that may themselves be generated, and errors introduced during compression arrive with no visible seam. Click through to whatever it is summarizing when the answer matters. #volatile
    - The circular-sourcing problem::Generated content cites other generated content, so a claim can appear in many places and have no original source at all. Repetition across sites is not corroboration — walk the chain back to a primary source or treat the claim as unsupported.
    - How AI changes the media-literacy rules you already have::It does not replace them, it raises the stakes. Check the source, prefer primary, notice your own wish for it to be true — all still hold. What changed is that fluency, correct formatting, and plausible detail are now free, so they carry no evidentiary weight.

- ## Talking to Kids About AI
    - How to explain an LLM to a young child::It is a very good guessing machine for what words come next, trained by reading an enormous amount of writing. It is not looking anything up, and it does not know when it is wrong.
    - The most important thing for a kid to understand about AI::It can be completely confident and completely wrong at the same time, which is different from most people they have met. Confidence is not a clue.
    - What a kid should do before believing something an AI said::Check it somewhere else, and prefer a source that has a name and something to lose. Two independent confirmations, not two websites that copied each other.
    - How to handle "is the AI alive?" from a kid::Honestly: it is not alive the way you are — no body, no feelings, no life going on when the conversation ends. Whether anything is happening inside it is a real question that smart people disagree about, and nobody knows for certain yet. Contested: #theory
    - Why a kid should still learn to write, calculate, and search::Because you cannot supervise a tool whose job you cannot do at all. The point of the skill is not that machines lack it; it is that judgment about the output requires it.

- ## Honest Open Questions
    - The labor question, stated honestly::Whether AI destroys more work than it creates, and on what timescale, is genuinely unresolved — economists disagree, historical analogies point in both directions, and confident predictions in either direction are not currently supported. Contested: #theory #volatile
    - The capability trajectory question, stated honestly::Whether current approaches keep improving toward general capability or hit a ceiling is unsettled, and both the loudest optimists and the loudest skeptics are asserting more than anyone can currently know. Contested: #theory #volatile
    - The right posture given the uncertainty::Build skills that compose with the tools rather than betting on a specific forecast — judgment, verification, taste, and knowing what question to ask are useful across every version of the outcome. This is antifragility applied to a technology, not a prediction.
