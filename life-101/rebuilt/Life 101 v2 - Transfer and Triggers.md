- ## Why This File Exists
    - The gap this file closes::The Foundational 200 teaches the thinking tools and Life Skills teaches the jobs, and across roughly 2,200 cards they almost never reference each other. A tool you cannot recall at the moment of use is not a tool. These cards weld the two halves together. #spine
    - The two card genres here, and what each does::Transfer cards carry a named model into a concrete situation where it bites. Trigger cards run the other direction — they start from a situation and name which models apply. Transfer teaches application; triggers teach recognition, which is the harder half. #spine

- ## Transfer — Sunk Cost
    - Sunk cost — where it bites in car repair::You have already put $2,000 into a failing transmission, which is exactly why the next $3,000 feels justified. The $2,000 is gone under every option. The only live question is whether $3,000 buys more remaining car than $3,000 spent on a different car. #spine
    - Sunk cost — where it bites in home renovation::A project 60% finished and over budget invites "we've come too far to stop." The finished 60% has whatever value it has regardless; decide the remaining 40% as if someone else had done the first part. #spine
    - Sunk cost — where it bites in a relationship or a career::"I've given this eight years" is a statement about the past presented as an argument about the future. The years are spent either way — the question is only whether the next year is worth its cost. #spine
    - Sunk cost — the tell that you are committing it::Any sentence containing "already" or "after all this" as a reason to continue. Those words point backward, and only forward-looking costs and benefits can justify a forward-looking decision. #spine

- ## Transfer — Base Rates and the Base-Rate Fallacy
    - Base rate — where it bites in reading a medical test result::A positive result on a highly accurate test for a rare condition is still usually a false positive, because the huge healthy population generates more false positives than the tiny sick population generates true ones. Ask the prevalence before you panic, and ask for the confirmatory test. #spine
    - Base rate — where it bites in home and car buying::Before believing "this one is different," ask how often that claim is true in general. Most cars at that price have that problem; most homes of that age have that issue. The specific story feels more informative than the statistic and almost never is. #spine
    - Base rate — where it bites in evaluating a business or investment pitch::Start from how often ventures of this type succeed, then adjust for what is genuinely unusual here. Starting from the story and never reaching the base rate is the single most expensive reasoning error in the deck. #spine
    - Base rate — the question that operationalizes it::"Out of a hundred situations that look like this one, how many turn out the way I am assuming?" If you cannot estimate that number even roughly, you do not yet have an opinion. #spine

- ## Transfer — Second-Order Thinking
    - Second-order thinking — where it bites in home repair decisions::A cheap fix that solves the visible symptom often accelerates the underlying failure — sealing a symptom of water intrusion traps the water. Ask what the repair causes, not only what it stops. #spine
    - Second-order thinking — where it bites in parenting::A consequence that stops a behavior today can teach a lesson you did not intend tomorrow — paying for grades can replace curiosity with price. Ask what the child learns about themselves, not just what they do next. #spine
    - Second-order thinking — where it bites in money decisions::Withdrawing retirement money early costs the tax and penalty and the decades of compounding that money would have done. The first-order cost is on the statement; the second-order cost is the one that matters. #spine
    - Second-order thinking — the four-word version::"And then what?" Ask it three times in a row about any decision and most bad ones reveal themselves. #spine

- ## Transfer — Opportunity Cost
    - Opportunity cost — where it bites in the pay-cash-or-finance decision::Paying cash for a car at a 3% loan rate costs you whatever that money would have earned invested. The real comparison is the spread between the two rates, which is exactly what the 4% rule encodes. #spine
    - Opportunity cost — where it bites in time and home maintenance::A Saturday of DIY that saves $200 costs the Saturday. The base's own DIY threshold — hire out when the savings are under about $500 and the risk is high — is an opportunity-cost calculation wearing work clothes. #spine
    - Opportunity cost — where it bites in saying yes to commitments::Every yes is a no to whatever would have occupied that time, including rest. The cost of a commitment is never the hours it takes; it is the best thing those hours would otherwise have held. #spine

- ## Transfer — Incentives
    - Incentives — where they bite when reading any price structure::A confusing structure is not an accident of history; it survived because confusion is profitable. Ask what the design makes hard to compare, and that is where the margin is hiding. #spine
    - Incentives — where they bite with any professional advising you::Ask how this person is paid before weighing the advice. Commission on the product recommended, a fee for the hour, or salary regardless — each produces a different reliable slant, and none of them requires anyone to be dishonest. #spine
    - Incentives — where they bite in your own household::You respond to incentives too. Environment design works because it changes what is rewarded moment to moment, which beats deciding to want different things. #spine
    - Incentives — the guardrail against over-applying them::Hanlon's Razor. Most bad outcomes are carelessness rather than design. Use incentives to explain persistent systemic patterns; use incompetence to explain individual events. #spine

- ## Transfer — Feedback Loops
    - Positive feedback loop — where it bites in personal finance::Credit card interest compounds against you while minimum payments barely touch principal, so the balance accelerates. Any process where the output feeds the input needs interrupting early, because it only gets harder later. #spine
    - Negative feedback loop — where it shows up as good design::Automatic transfers, thermostats, budgets with categories, and a scheduled check-in with a friend are all correction mechanisms that fire without requiring you to notice the drift. #spine
    - Feedback loops — the diagnostic question for any recurring problem::"Is something here feeding itself?" If yes, willpower applied at the current point will fail — you have to break the loop or add a damper, not push harder against it. #spine
    - Feedback loops — where the delay is the real problem::Long delays between action and consequence (health, home maintenance, savings, climate) defeat learning, because the feedback arrives too late to be attached to the cause. That delay is why those domains need schedules rather than intuition. #spine

- ## Transfer — Survivorship, Availability, and Anchoring
    - Survivorship bias — where it bites in advice-taking::Advice comes from the people whose approach worked, and you never hear from the identical people it failed. Ask what happened to everyone who did the same thing, then discount accordingly. #spine
    - Survivorship bias — where it bites in home and car buying::"They don't make them like they used to" is partly true and mostly selection — the badly-made ones are gone. The survivors are the survivors. #spine
    - Availability heuristic — where it bites in risk decisions::Vivid risks feel likely and quiet ones feel remote, so people fear the dramatic hazard while skipping the screening and the smoke-alarm battery. Check the base rate against the feeling, every time. #spine
    - Anchoring — where it bites in every negotiation and purchase::The first number spoken sets the range regardless of its basis, which is why sticker prices, list prices, and opening offers are chosen strategically. Decide your own number before you hear theirs. #spine
    - Anchoring — where it bites in a medical bill or repair quote::The quoted total is an anchor, not a fact — the itemized breakdown is where the real numbers live, and the base's audit sequence exists to get you past the anchor. #spine

- ## Transfer — Expected Value and Regression to the Mean
    - Expected value — where it bites in insurance decisions::Insurance is a negative-expected-value bet you should still take when the loss would be unrecoverable. Insure catastrophes, self-insure inconveniences — which is why raising a deductible you can comfortably cover usually wins. #spine
    - Expected value — where it bites in extended warranties::Priced to be profitable for the seller, which means negative expected value for you, and covering a loss you could absorb. Decline by default; the exception is a loss you genuinely could not cover. #spine
    - Regression to the mean — where it bites in judging what worked::An unusually bad stretch tends to improve on its own, so whatever you tried right after it gets undeserved credit. This is why punishment feels more effective than it is and praise feels less. #spine
    - Regression to the mean — where it bites in health and performance::Symptoms and slumps often peak before they resolve, so the remedy applied at the worst moment inherits the recovery. Compare against what would have happened anyway. #spine

- ## Transfer — Map and Territory, Occam, Via Negativa, Antifragility
    - Map and territory — where it bites in trusting any plan or budget::The budget is not the money and the plan is not the project. When they disagree with reality, reality is not making an error. Update the map. #spine
    - Map and territory — where it bites in emergency planning::A route on paper that you have never driven is a hypothesis. The base's own instruction to physically drive an evacuation route once a year is exactly this correction. #spine
    - Occam's razor — where it bites in diagnosing anything::Before the elaborate explanation, exhaust the boring ones — is it plugged in, is the filter dirty, did anyone check the breaker. Most failures have dull causes, and the dull causes are cheap to test. #spine
    - Via negativa — where it bites in health and productivity::Removal usually beats addition. Cutting the late caffeine, the extra commitment, or the second screen outperforms adding a supplement, a system, or an app — and subtraction has no maintenance cost. #spine
    - Antifragility — where it bites in how you structure savings and skills::Build so that shocks make you stronger rather than merely surviving them: an emergency fund that turns a layoff into an opportunity, redundant skills, multiple income sources. The goal is not to predict the shock but to be improved by it. #spine
    - Skin in the game — where it bites in whose advice to weight::Weight the opinion of someone who bears the cost of being wrong. The contractor who warranties the work, the doctor who follows up, the advisor paid on your outcome rather than your transaction. #spine

- ## Transfer — Pareto, Tragedy of the Commons, Confirmation Bias
    - Pareto principle — where it bites in home maintenance and health::A small number of items produce most of the risk — roof, water, electrical panel, and drainage in a house; sleep, movement, and not smoking in a body. Do those completely before optimizing anything else. #spine
    - Tragedy of the commons — where it bites inside a household::Shared resources with no owner degrade — the fridge, the garage, the group chat, the shared calendar. Assign a named owner or accept the decay; there is no third option. #spine
    - Confirmation bias — where it bites in a major purchase::Once you have chosen, you start collecting evidence you were right and stop seeing the rest. The correction is to write down, before deciding, what would make you walk away — then check that list. #spine
    - Confirmation bias — where it bites in health and money research::Searching for a conclusion always finds it. Search for the strongest case against your plan first; if it survives that, it is worth doing. #spine

- ## Triggers — Money and Purchases
    - You are in the dealership finance office and they have started talking in monthly payments. What applies?::1) De-bundle — settle total price first, then the trade-in, then financing, each separately. 2) The 4% rule (car financing) — under 4% finance and keep the cash invested, above it pay cash. 3) Anchoring — the sticker and the first payment quoted are chosen, not derived. The bundle is the trap. #spine
    - You are about to buy a used car and the seller says it runs great. What applies?::1) The pre-purchase inspection at $100-200 — you are buying information, not labor. 2) Base rates — how often do cars of this age and model have this problem? 3) The used-car sweet spot of 2-4 years old. Never skip the inspection to save the fee. #spine
    - A repair quote just came in and it is large. What applies?::1) The 50% Rule — over half of replacement cost, replace instead. 2) For HVAC specifically, the $5,000 rule — age in years times the repair quote. 3) Sunk cost — money already spent on this machine is not an argument. 4) Get a second quote; the first is an anchor. #spine
    - You are deciding whether to rent or buy a home. What applies?::1) The 5% rule (rent vs buy) — if annual rent is under 5% of purchase price, renting generally wins. 2) The 10-year rule (buying) — buy as though you will stay ten years. 3) Opportunity cost — the down payment could be earning elsewhere. 4) Hidden costs of ownership at roughly 1% maintenance plus 1-2% tax plus 0.5-1% insurance annually. #spine
    - You just got a raise or a windfall. What applies?::1) The canonical money order — find your current step and fund it, do not skip ahead. 2) Hedonic adaptation — lifestyle inflation absorbs raises invisibly, so automate the increase into savings before it lands in checking. 3) The Rule of 72 on the amount, to feel what it becomes if left alone. #spine
    - A market drop has just wiped out a chunk of your retirement balance. What applies?::1) The emergency fund exists precisely so you never sell into a drop — check that it is intact first. 2) Regression to the mean and the law of large numbers — short runs say nothing. 3) The availability heuristic — the crash is vivid and recent, which is exactly why it feels like a trend. Do nothing on the day you feel most like doing something. #spine
    - Someone is pitching you an investment with an unusually high return. What applies?::1) Base rates for that category of venture. 2) Incentives — how is the person pitching it paid? 3) Skin in the game — do they hold the same position on the same terms? 4) Expected value including the probability of total loss, not just the advertised upside. #spine
    - You are choosing a health insurance plan for next year. What applies?::1) The break-even calculation — annual premium difference against deductible difference. 2) The rough utilization threshold: above about $5,000 of expected care, the low-deductible plan usually wins; well under it, the high-deductible plan plus an HSA usually does. 3) Check whether your actual doctors are in network — insurer directories are frequently out of date. #spine

- ## Triggers — Health and Safety
    - Someone is showing sudden weakness, facial droop, or garbled speech. What applies?::Stroke recognition — FAST. Face, Arm, Speech, and Time: note the exact minute symptoms began, because treatment windows are measured from onset. Call 911 immediately; do not wait to see if it passes and do not drive them yourself. #safety #spine
    - Someone collapses and is not breathing normally. What applies?::1) Call 911 and send someone for an AED. 2) Hands-only CPR — center of the chest, about 100-120 compressions per minute, about two inches deep, minimal interruptions. 3) Do not stop to check for a pulse repeatedly. Imperfect compressions vastly outperform none. #safety #spine
    - There is a fire and you are deciding whether to fight it. What applies?::1) The fight-or-flee rule — anything bigger than a wastebasket, leave. 2) The PASS technique if you do fight it: Pull, Aim at the base, Squeeze, Sweep. 3) Keep an exit at your back, always. 4) Close doors behind you as you go. #safety #spine
    - You are trying to decide between the ER, urgent care, and telehealth. What applies?::ER for chest pain, stroke signs, severe bleeding, difficulty breathing, or head injury with confusion. Urgent care for stitches, sprains, minor burns, and infections. Telehealth for rashes, refills, and triage when you are unsure. Cost differences are large, but never let cost delay the first category. #safety #spine
    - A medical bill arrives and it is much larger than expected. What applies?::1) The medical bill audit sequence — itemized bill first, then match line-by-line against the EOB. 2) Anchoring — the summary total is a starting position. 3) The appeal ladder if the denial is the problem. 4) Ask about the cash-pay rate and financial assistance; both are frequently unadvertised. #spine
    - You are cooking and unsure whether food is safe. What applies?::1) The Danger Zone — 40 to 140°F, bacteria doubling roughly every 20 minutes. 2) Two hours maximum at room temperature, one hour above 90°F. 3) Two-stage cooling for leftovers, and reheat to 165°F. 4) When genuinely unsure, discard — the expected value strongly favors throwing food away. #safety #spine
    - You smell gas, or the air outside has turned smoky. What applies?::Gas: leave first, call from outside, never operate switches, and a professional must relight. Smoke: check AQI thresholds, close up and recirculate, run filtration, and treat a red-flag warning day as one where you keep the go-bag and half tank ready. #safety #spine

- ## Triggers — Emergencies and Evacuation
    - Conditions are ambiguous and you are not sure whether to evacuate. What applies?::The 60% rule — leave at 60% certainty rather than 95%, because early departure is the single biggest predictor of good outcomes and the road capacity vanishes exactly when everyone becomes certain. Ambiguity is the signal to go, not the reason to wait. #safety #spine
    - The ground has just stopped shaking. What applies?::1) Drop, Cover, Hold On is for during; now expect aftershocks. 2) Shoes before walking anywhere — broken glass. 3) Check for injuries, then gas only if you smell or hear a leak. 4) Text rather than call; networks congest. 5) Then, and only then, consult the route plan. #safety #spine
    - You are leaving in a hurry and have two minutes to grab things. What applies?::1) People and pets, then medication, then the document set. 2) The three homes of every critical document means the originals should already be handled — if you are improvising this, the pre-commitment failed. 3) The half-tank rule means you should already have the range to get out. #safety #spine
    - Your phone or laptop just died, was stolen, or was compromised. What applies?::1) The 3-2-1 backup rule tells you whether this is an inconvenience or a catastrophe — and the quarterly restore-test is what makes the answer trustworthy. 2) Rotate credentials from a clean device. 3) Freeze credit at all three bureaus if identity data was exposed. #spine
    - A message or call is pressuring you to act immediately about money or a family emergency. What applies?::1) Urgency is the manipulation, in essentially every scam. 2) Hang up and call back on a number you already have. 3) Use the family verification password for any claimed emergency — voice cloning makes a familiar voice worthless as identification. 4) No legitimate institution requires gift cards, crypto, or wire transfers under time pressure. #safety #spine

- ## Triggers — People
    - A conversation is turning hot and you can feel it. What applies?::1) The 20-minute rule — if it has run that long without progress, break and reconvene. 2) Watch for kitchen sinking; hold to one issue. 3) State facts and impact ("when X happened, I felt Y") rather than character ("you always"). 4) Physiological flooding is real — you are not able to reason well right now, and neither are they. #spine
    - You need to raise something difficult with someone. What applies?::1) The memorized opener: "I need to discuss something important. Is now good?" 2) Choose the moment while calm, not while triggered. 3) Know your specific desired outcome before starting. 4) RASA when it is their turn — Receive, Appreciate, Summarize, Ask. #spine
    - Someone has crossed a line with you. What applies?::1) The Boundary Formula — state the boundary, explain briefly, offer an alternative, enforce the consequence. 2) Consistency is the mechanism; inconsistent enforcement teaches that violation works. 3) A boundary without a consequence is a request. #spine
    - You were the one who was wrong. What applies?::1) Name specifically what you did, without "but" and without "if you felt." 2) State the impact you caused rather than your intention. 3) Offer repair, then state the changed behavior. 4) An apology that explains itself is a defense wearing an apology's clothes. #spine
    - A friendship has gone quiet and you are not sure it is still alive. What applies?::1) Hours-to-friendship thresholds — closeness is built and maintained by time, not sentiment. 2) Contact every 2-4 weeks is the maintenance interval, and this is Pattern 1 in social form. 3) The person who reaches out is usually not the one being neglected; assume nothing from silence. #spine
    - Someone tells you they are in crisis. What applies?::1) Ask directly and plainly, including about suicide — asking does not plant the idea. 2) Listen without fixing. 3) Do not promise secrecy you cannot keep. 4) 988 in the US, by call or text, and stay with them through the handoff. #safety #spine
    - You are walking into a social event where you know almost no one. What applies?::1) The FORD method for small talk — Family, Occupation, Recreation, Dreams. 2) Have your exit line ready in advance. 3) The spotlight effect — nobody is tracking you nearly as closely as it feels. #spine
    - You have been offered a job or a raise and a number is on the table. What applies?::1) BATNA — know your walk-away before the conversation, because it is the only real source of leverage. 2) Anchoring — avoid naming the first number when you can. 3) Negotiate the whole package, not just salary. 4) Silence after an offer is a tool, not an awkwardness. #spine

- ## Triggers — Habits, Work, and Parenting
    - You have missed a day of a habit you are building. What applies?::1) The two-day rule — never miss twice; one miss is an accident, two starts a new habit. 2) All-or-nothing thinking is the actual threat, not the missed day. 3) Shrink tomorrow's version below the resistance threshold rather than compensating with a bigger one. #spine
    - You keep failing at something despite genuinely intending to do it. What applies?::1) The meta-pattern — stop buying more willpower and change the structure. 2) Environment design — make it obvious and easy, or invisible and hard. 3) Implementation intentions — name the time, the place, and the specific action. If the fix lives only in your intentions, it is not a fix. #spine
    - You are estimating how long a project will take. What applies?::1) The planning fallacy — your estimate is systematically optimistic. 2) Reference-class forecasting — how long did similar things actually take, including the ones that went badly? 3) Second-order thinking on what a slip cascades into. #spine
    - You are procrastinating and cannot make yourself start. What applies?::1) Procrastination is emotion regulation, not laziness — name what the task actually makes you feel. 2) Shrink the first action until it is smaller than the resistance. 3) Self-compassion beats self-criticism here; rumination reliably increases avoidance. #spine
    - A kid has asked you a question you cannot answer. What applies?::1) Say "I don't know" and mean it — modeling that is worth more than the answer. 2) Then find out together, which teaches the method rather than the fact. 3) Negate the folk belief first if there is one, then give the mechanism. 4) On values questions, give the map of positions rather than a verdict. #spine
    - A kid asks something where the honest answer is uncomfortable. What applies?::1) Answer the question actually asked, at the level asked, rather than the one you fear. 2) Label epistemic status honestly — "we think," "nobody knows," "people disagree." 3) Scare tactics fail on contact with reality, so accuracy is the safer long-run strategy. #spine
    - You are about to sign a contract, lease, or agreement. What applies?::1) The escalation rule — get a lawyer for real estate, employment agreements, anything over $10K, equity, and anything you do not fully understand. 2) Never sign with blank spaces. 3) Look specifically for automatic renewal, unilateral termination, and waived rights. 4) "Standard" is a sales word, not a legal one. #spine
